# Calibration-transport family and anchor ladder

This note is the normative theory bridge for the boundary paper. It closes the stable-calibration, bounded-drift and unrestricted-drift cases into one parameter family and makes the source of the sensitivity bound explicit.

## 1. Symmetric calibration-transport family

Let

```text
W_i = F_i E_i,
X_i = q_i F_i,
kappa = q_1/q_0,
rho_W = W_1/W_0,
rho_X = X_1/X_0.
```

Then

```text
rho_F = rho_X/kappa,
rho_E = (rho_W/rho_X) kappa.
```

Write `rho_E_hat=rho_W/rho_X` for the stable-calibration value. Define the multiplicatively symmetric transport bound

```text
1/Gamma <= kappa <= Gamma,   Gamma >= 1.
```

Equivalently, with `eta=log Gamma`,

```text
|log kappa| <= eta.
```

This gives one calibration-transport family:

| transport bound | identification state | interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| `Gamma=1` | point identified | N3: stable calibration |
| `1<Gamma<infinity` | sharply partially identified | bounded calibration drift |
| `Gamma -> infinity` | non-identified | N4: unrestricted positive calibration drift |

Thus N3 and N4 are not separate algebraic curiosities. They are the stable and unrestricted endpoints of the same transport family.

For finite `Gamma`, the complementary channel satisfies

```text
rho_E in [rho_E_hat/Gamma, rho_E_hat*Gamma].
```

The corresponding fecundity interval is generated by the same `kappa`, so the sharp joint set remains

```text
rho_F rho_E = rho_W.
```

In log-ratio coordinates it is a slope `-1` line segment.

## 2. Symmetric breakdown factor

A directional conclusion first fails when the identified interval reaches one. Under the symmetric family,

```text
Gamma_star = max(rho_hat, 1/rho_hat)
eta_star = |log rho_hat|.
```

This is invariant to reversing the reference regime. For example,

```text
rho_hat = 1/1.34  -> Gamma_star = 1.34
rho_hat = 1.34    -> Gamma_star = 1.34
```

The former manuscript wording `34% calibration drift` remains a useful directional translation for the decline example because `kappa=1.34` is the boundary that overturns the decline. It should not be the primary mathematical robustness scale because the additive-around-one parameterisation is not invariant to regime reversal. The primary report should be the distortion factor `Gamma_star=1.34`, optionally accompanied by `eta_star=log(1.34)` and the reader-friendly statement that this corresponds to a 34% upward calibration-ratio shift in the worked direction.

## 3. Why Gamma is not estimated from the same data

A finite `Gamma` is not identified from the same net-response and proxy observations whose identifying power is under study. Bounded-drift analysis does not claim otherwise. Its purpose is to expose how strongly a biological conclusion depends on an externally supplied transport tolerance.

The breakdown factor reverses the usual burden of specification. Rather than asking the analyst to defend one uniquely correct value of `Gamma`, it reports the smallest multiplicative calibration distortion sufficient to overturn the conclusion. Readers can then compare that threshold with independent calibration experiments, instrument knowledge, biological prior information, or direct anchor measurements.

Accordingly, the software must never infer `Gamma`, `eta`, or the legacy `delta` from the same `W` and `X` observations used in the identification analysis.

## 4. Anchor ladder

The source of the calibration restriction can be stated as a measurement-design ladder. This ladder concerns direct calibration anchors across regimes.

| direct calibration anchors | what is learned | identification consequence |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | no direct information on cross-regime proxy conversion | with unrestricted `kappa`, N4 non-identification |
| 1 | local `q` is anchored in one regime; cross-regime transport remains unknown | an external finite `Gamma` yields a sharp identified set and breakdown factor |
| 2 | `q_0=X_0/F_0` and `q_1=X_1/F_1` are both observed, so `kappa=q_1/q_0` is measured | point identification; no sensitivity bound is required |

This is a refinement of Design Rule 1, not a claim that a relative proxy can never be analysed without anchors. Stable transport may always be imposed as an assumption (`Gamma=1`). The ladder instead makes explicit what direct measurement buys: one anchor localises the calibration problem; two anchors measure the transport ratio itself.

### Design Rule 1 — Anchor and transport, graded form

Choose the number of direct calibration anchors according to the strength of inference required.

- With no direct transport calibration, treat cross-regime conversion as an assumption and do not claim channel identification under unrestricted drift.
- With one anchor, combine the local calibration with a prespecified external transport bound and report the sharp joint identified set plus its breakdown factor.
- With two anchors, estimate both local conversion factors and use the observed `kappa` for point identification.

The progression is therefore

```text
measurement effort
0 anchors -> 1 anchor -> 2 anchors

identification strength
non-identification -> partial identification -> point identification
```

under the corresponding transport-information conditions.

## 5. N1 as a group action and quotient statement

Let `G` be the multiplicative group of positive functions `c(z)>0`, acting on latent decompositions by

```text
c . (F,E) = (cF, E/c).
```

The net response

```text
W=FE
```

is invariant under this action. Therefore every net-only observable `Phi(W)` is constant on each orbit of `G` and factors through the quotient of latent decompositions by this action.

This is the structural reason the complete performance curve, every threshold-feasible set `Omega_t`, and all geometry or topology derived from them cannot identify the allocation between `F` and `E`. The concrete list should remain in the manuscript for ecological intuition; the group-action sentence shows that the closure is structural rather than an item-by-item enumeration.

## 6. Reporting rule remains joint

The symmetric transport family does not alter Design Rule 2. One common `kappa` generates both channel ratios, so marginal intervals are projections of a one-dimensional joint set, not independently combinable uncertainties. In log-ratio coordinates the calibration-drift component remains exactly slope `-1`.

## 7. Backward compatibility with the legacy delta display

The existing API based on

```text
kappa in [1-delta, 1+delta]
```

is retained for reproducibility and for the worked 34% directional illustration. It should be described as a directional/additive-around-one display, not as the canonical scale for comparing robustness across reversed contrasts.

For new theory statements and cross-study comparison, use `Gamma` or `eta`.
