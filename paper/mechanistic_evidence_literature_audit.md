# Mechanistic-evidence literature audit

Status: submission-facing source audit for the conceptual framing of the boundary Perspective.

## Audit question

The paper separates two properties of ecological evidence:

```text
biological measurement level / mechanistic proximity
identification strength among declared competing mechanisms
```

The literature audit asks what can be claimed about that distinction without caricaturing molecular ecology, observational ecology or causal-inference work.

## Result in one sentence

The literature supports the coexistence of two mechanistic aspirations—proximity to biological machinery and discrimination/characterisation of causal processes—but does **not** justify claiming that ecology formally endorses a universal one-dimensional hierarchy in which molecular data are intrinsically more mechanistic than field data.

The manuscript should therefore say that the two meanings are often *conflated or left implicit*, not that the field has adopted an explicit universal hierarchy.

## 1. Ecological genomics positively supports the proximity axis

### Ungerer, Johnson & Herman 2008 — Heredity

**Citation:** Ungerer, M.C., Johnson, L.C. & Herman, M.A. 2008. Ecological genomics: understanding gene and genome function in the natural environment. *Heredity* 100:178–183. doi:10.1038/sj.hdy.6800992.

**What it establishes:** ecological genomics explicitly seeks to understand genetic mechanisms underlying organismal responses to natural environments. Genomic and functional-genomic measurements are therefore legitimately described as mechanistically proximal and can connect ecological phenotypes to genes and pathways.

**What it does not establish:** that genomic measurement level alone identifies a unique ecological mechanism, or that field observations are necessarily non-mechanistic.

### Rudman et al. 2018 — Nature Ecology & Evolution

**Citation:** Rudman, S.M., Barbour, M.A., Csilléry, K. et al. 2018. What genomic data can reveal about eco-evolutionary dynamics. *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 2:9–15. doi:10.1038/s41559-017-0385-2.

**What it establishes:** genomic data can deepen mechanistic understanding of phenotypic change and eco-evolutionary dynamics.

**Critical qualification from the paper:** genomic data alone are not sufficient to resolve the major eco-evolutionary questions considered. This is unusually clean support for the present distinction: mechanistic proximity and mechanistic information can increase without guaranteeing identification of the whole ecological explanation.

**Safe use:** cite Rudman as an ally, not as an example of an error. Their own qualification already anticipates the distinction.

## 2. Ecology already recognises that causal mechanisms have distinct evidentiary requirements

### Grace et al. 2025 — Ecology Letters

**Citation:** Grace, J.B., Huntington-Klein, N., Schweiger, E.W. et al. 2025. Causal effects versus causal mechanisms: two traditions with different requirements and contributions towards causal understanding. *Ecology Letters* 28:e70029. doi:10.1111/ele.70029.

**What it establishes:** causal-effect estimation and causal-mechanistic investigation answer different questions and have different evidentiary requirements. Mechanistic investigations seek to characterise the structures and processes conveying causal effects.

**Relation to this paper:** the boundary Perspective is narrower. It does not redefine all mechanistic explanation as identification. It asks, conditional on declared alternatives and an observation map, whether the available observations distinguish the latent mechanisms or channel decompositions relevant to the claim.

**Safe positioning sentence:** “We add an identification axis to mechanistic evidence: proximity to a process and evidence that distinguishes competing process explanations are related but non-equivalent properties.”

## 3. Intermediate processes require design and explicit assumptions, not endpoint association alone

### Correia, Dee & Ferraro 2025 — Biological Reviews

**Citation:** Correia, H.E., Dee, L.E. & Ferraro, P.J. 2025. Designing causal mediation analyses to quantify intermediary processes in ecology. *Biological Reviews* 100:1512–1533. doi:10.1111/brv.70011.

**What it establishes:** ecological explanations often concern intermediary processes; valid inference about those processes requires explicit definitions, causal assumptions and suitable design. Causal assumptions cannot generally be verified from the same data, and sensitivity analysis is part of responsible inference.

**Relation to this paper:** this independently supports two features of the boundary framework: missing links should be treated as design problems rather than silently inferred from endpoints, and external assumptions should be exposed through sensitivity/breakdown analysis rather than hidden.

**Do not claim:** that the present product theorem is a mediation theorem or estimates mediated causal effects.

## 4. Field-level evidence can become mechanistic through observation design

### Smith et al. 2020 — Journal of Animal Ecology

**Citation:** Smith, J.A., Suraci, J.P., Hunter, J.S. et al. 2020. Zooming in on mechanistic predator–prey ecology: integrating camera traps with experimental methods to reveal the drivers of ecological interactions. *Journal of Animal Ecology* 89:1997–2012. doi:10.1111/1365-2656.13264.

**What it establishes:** large observational camera-trap datasets can describe spatiotemporal outcomes while remaining limited for identifying mechanistic drivers; field experiments using the same broad observational technology can test hypothesised mechanisms under natural conditions.

**Why it matters:** this gives a concrete ecological counterexample to a biological-level hierarchy. The difference in mechanistic strength comes from design and discriminatory structure, not from moving to a molecular level.

**Do not claim:** that experiments are always necessary for identification. Observational designs can identify causal effects or mechanisms under suitable assumptions; the narrower point is that field-level data are not intrinsically restricted to “pattern”.

## 5. Observational ecology can support causal inference when the design identifies the target

### Siegel & Dee 2025 — Ecology Letters

**Citation:** Siegel, K. & Dee, L.E. 2025. Foundations and future directions for causal inference in ecological research. *Ecology Letters* 28:e70053. doi:10.1111/ele.70053.

**What it establishes:** ecology has a long experimental tradition, but modern observational data streams can answer causal questions when paired with explicit causal design and assumptions.

**Relation to this paper:** measurement surface alone does not determine inferential status. This paper's target is different—structural identification of mechanisms/channels rather than causal-effect estimation—but the design-first logic is compatible.

## 6. What the literature does and does not license

### Licensed claims

- Genomic and molecular measurements can provide genuine mechanistic proximity and important mechanistic information.
- Mechanistic proximity does not by itself imply that a unique mechanism has been distinguished.
- Field observations can be designed or combined with interventions so that they test mechanisms rather than merely describe patterns.
- Inference about intermediary processes depends on observation design and assumptions.
- A measurement should be evaluated relative to the scientific target and competing explanations, not by technological depth alone.

### Claims to avoid

- “Ecology believes molecular data are mechanism and field data are pattern.”
- “Molecular data are not mechanistic.”
- “Genomics cannot identify mechanisms.”
- “Field observations are generally more mechanistic than molecular data.”
- “Biological measurement level and identification strength are statistically independent.”
- “All mechanistic evidence reduces to point identification.”

## 7. Terminology decision

Avoid **orthogonal** as the primary adjective. The two dimensions are conceptually distinct and neither orders the other, but they may be empirically correlated in particular research programmes. Use:

```text
two distinct axes
non-equivalent properties
no monotone relation is implied
```

rather than claiming statistical or geometric independence.

## 8. Manuscript consequence

The conceptual sequence should be:

```text
"mechanistic" has multiple legitimate ecological uses
-> biological proximity and identification strength are non-equivalent
-> current product/proxy theorems provide quantitative cases where the difference is exact
-> channel/calibration measurements change identification by changing the observation map
-> reporting should track the resulting identified set, not the prestige or depth of the measurement technology
```

The product and proxy results remain the formal core. The broader two-axis statement is a Perspective-level synthesis supported by the literature and worked theorems, not a universal theorem over every possible ecological observation map.
