# From point identification to bounded-drift partial identification in multiplicative ecological channels

> **Boundary-paper draft.** This manuscript is separate from the RACH observation-selection submission. It develops the N1–N4 identifiability boundary, the bounded calibration-drift identified set, breakdown points and measurement-design rules.

## Abstract

Ecologists often observe a net response generated by several positive vital-rate or service channels. When total trait performance is the product `W(z)=F(z)E(z)`, a change in `W` alone cannot identify whether `F`, `E`, or both changed, even if the full performance curve is observed without sampling error. We formalise this boundary. Net-only observations are structurally non-identifying (N1), while net performance plus either exact channel identifies the other (N2). A proxy `X_i=q_iF_i` identifies relative channel change when its conversion is stable across regimes (N3), whereas unconstrained conversion drift restores non-identifiability (N4).

The practically important intermediate case is bounded calibration drift. If `kappa=q_1/q_0` lies in `[1-delta,1+delta]`, the complementary-channel ratio has the sharp identified set `[rho_hat(1-delta),rho_hat(1+delta)]`, with multiplicative width `(1+delta)/(1-delta)`. This yields a direct breakdown point for directional claims. A conclusion that the complementary channel decreased is robust while `rho_hat(1+delta)<1`; if `rho_hat<1`, the calibration-drift breakdown point is `delta*=1/rho_hat-1`. The increasing case is symmetric.

These results convert a generic warning about imperfect proxies into an operational sensitivity analysis. We give design rules that distinguish when channel attribution is forbidden, point-identified, partially identified, or recoverable only by additional calibration. The universal object is not a particular ecological mechanism but the information lost when channels are collapsed into a net product.

## 1. Introduction

Many ecological conclusions are expressed at the level of mechanisms even though observations are collected at the level of net outcomes. Recruitment can be decomposed into propagule production and establishment. Effective mutualistic service can be decomposed into encounter rate and per-encounter effectiveness. Fitness can be written as the product of survival, reproduction and recruitment components over a declared census interval. In each case, the same net response may be compatible with different channel changes.

This is not merely a problem of sampling uncertainty. More replication can estimate the net response with increasing precision while leaving the channel decomposition unresolved. The relevant question is structural: does the observation map preserve enough information to distinguish the latent channels?

We study the positive two-channel model

```text
W_i(z)=F_i(z)E_i(z),    F_i(z)>0, E_i(z)>0,
```

for regimes `i in {0,1}` and trait or state value `z`. The positivity condition permits channel ratios and isolates the information loss caused by multiplication. The model is deliberately narrow. It is useful only when the ecological output and census interval have been declared so that the factorisation is meaningful.

The first four results define the endpoints of the boundary. N1 shows that every observation depending only on `W` is unable to identify the changed channel. N2 shows that exact measurement of either channel is sufficient. N3 shows why an absolutely uncalibrated proxy can nevertheless identify relative change if its conversion is stable. N4 shows that arbitrary conversion drift destroys that identification.

The main advance is to fill the gap between N3 and N4. Field investigators often cannot establish exact stability, but they may be able to defend a bound on calibration drift. This creates a partial-identification problem rather than an all-or-nothing one. We derive the sharp identified set, its width, directional robustness and the calibration-drift magnitude at which a scientific conclusion fails. The result changes the practical output from `the proxy may differ` to `the conclusion survives calibration drift up to delta*`.

## 2. Model and observation classes

### 2.1 Net-only observations

Let a net-only observation be any deterministic operator

```text
O_i = Phi(W_i).
```

This includes direct observation of the full `W_i(z)` curve and any transformation of that curve: thresholded viable sets, lower and upper edges, breadth, connected components, maxima, integrals or summaries. If two causal programs produce identical `W_i` pointwise, every such observation is identical.

### 2.2 Exact channel observations

An exact channel observation supplies either `F_i(z)` or `E_i(z)` on the same domain and comparison scale as `W_i(z)`. Exact means exact with respect to the declared mathematical channel, not free of sampling error. Statistical uncertainty in a measured channel can be propagated after the structural map is declared.

### 2.3 Proxy observations

Suppose the measured assay is

```text
X_i(z)=q_i(z)F_i(z),
```

where `q_i(z)>0` is the conversion from the assay to the mathematical channel. Examples include a visit count used as a proxy for successful reproduction or a connectivity index used as a proxy for reachability. The interpretation depends on both the proxy channel and the comparison-specific conversion.

Define the observed ratios

```text
rho_W = W_1/W_0,
rho_X = X_1/X_0,
```

and calibration drift

```text
kappa=q_1/q_0.
```

Then

```text
rho_X=kappa rho_F,
rho_W=rho_F rho_E.
```

## 3. Exact identification boundary

### 3.1 N1: net-only non-identifiability

**Theorem N1.** For every positive baseline pair `(F_0,E_0)` and positive trait-dependent multiplier `a(z)`, the distinct programs

```text
P_F: (F_1,E_1)=(aF_0,E_0)
P_E: (F_1,E_1)=(F_0,aE_0)
```

produce the same net outcome

```text
W_1=aF_0E_0.
```

Therefore no net-only observation `Phi(W_1)` can distinguish the programs.

**Proof.** Multiplication is commutative:

```text
(aF_0)E_0=F_0(aE_0).
```

The equality holds pointwise in `z`; applying any deterministic operator `Phi` preserves equality. ∎

N1 is stronger than a statement that the data are noisy or the sample is small. It constructs observationally equivalent causal worlds. Complete knowledge of all thresholded geometries does not recover the discarded channel label.

### 3.2 N2: one exact channel is sufficient

**Theorem N2.** If `W_i` and `F_i` are observed on the positive domain, then `E_i=W_i/F_i` is unique. Symmetrically, observing `W_i` and `E_i` gives `F_i=W_i/E_i`.

Consequently, both before/after channel ratios are identified:

```text
rho_F=F_1/F_0,
rho_E=(W_1/W_0)/(F_1/F_0).
```

No assumption that only one channel changed is required. The data can support fecundity-only, establishment-only, mixed or unchanged transitions.

### 3.3 N3: stable proxy conversion

**Theorem N3.** If `q_1(z)=q_0(z)>0`, then

```text
rho_F=rho_X
```

and

```text
rho_E=rho_W/rho_X.
```

Absolute calibration may remain unknown and trait-dependent. Only the regime ratio of the conversion must equal one. This is why a proxy can identify relative change even when it does not identify an absolute channel level.

### 3.4 N4: unconstrained drift

**Theorem N4.** If `kappa=q_1/q_0` is unrestricted over positive values, the same observed `rho_W` and `rho_X` are compatible with

```text
rho_F=rho_X/kappa,
rho_E=(rho_W/rho_X)kappa
```

for every positive `kappa`. The channel ratios and even their directions can therefore vary while observations remain fixed.

N4 does not say proxies are useless. It says a proxy is informative about a channel only through an explicit constraint on its conversion.

## 4. Bounded calibration drift

### 4.1 Sharp identified sets

Assume

```text
1-delta <= kappa <= 1+delta,    0<=delta<1.
```

Let

```text
rho_E_hat=rho_W/rho_X
```

be the N3 point estimate obtained under exact stability. Retaining `kappa` in the N3 division gives

```text
rho_F=rho_X/kappa,
rho_E=rho_E_hat kappa.
```

Hence

```text
rho_F in [rho_X/(1+delta), rho_X/(1-delta)]
```

and

```text
rho_E in [rho_E_hat(1-delta), rho_E_hat(1+delta)].
```

These sets are sharp: every point is generated by an admissible value of `kappa`. Their multiplicative width is

```text
(1+delta)/(1-delta).
```

The width does not depend on the observed point ratio. It depends only on how much calibration drift is allowed. The location of the interval depends on the observed data.

### 4.2 Directional robustness

A directional conclusion is identified when the entire interval lies on one side of one.

For the complementary channel:

```text
E decreased  iff  rho_E_hat(1+delta)<1,
E increased  iff  rho_E_hat(1-delta)>1.
```

For the proxied channel:

```text
F decreased  iff  rho_X/(1-delta)<1,
F increased  iff  rho_X/(1+delta)>1.
```

If the interval includes one, the magnitude remains partially identified but the direction does not.

### 4.3 Breakdown points

For `rho_E_hat<1`, the decrease conclusion reaches its boundary when

```text
rho_E_hat(1+delta*)=1,
```

so

```text
delta*=1/rho_E_hat-1.
```

For `rho_E_hat>1`, the increase conclusion breaks at

```text
delta*=1-1/rho_E_hat.
```

The proxied-channel formulas are `delta*=1-rho_X` for a decrease and `delta*=rho_X-1` for an increase. Breakdowns at or above one are censored by the admissible family `delta<1` and mean that the sign survives every allowed bound in that family.

### 4.4 Numerical illustration

Take

```text
rho_E_hat=1/1.34=0.746268...
```

Then

```text
delta*=0.34.
```

The conclusion `E decreased` is retained for calibration drift below 34%. At 34%, the upper endpoint equals one. This is the kind of statement a field paper can report: not that calibration is certainly stable, but how large a departure from stability is required to overturn the directional conclusion.

## 5. Observation-design rules

The theorems imply a simple decision sequence.

1. **Only net performance observed.** Do not attribute the response to a channel. More precision in `W` cannot break N1.
2. **Net performance and one exact channel observed.** Reconstruct the other channel and propagate measurement uncertainty under N2.
3. **Net performance and a proxy with defended stability.** Report relative channel changes under N3 and state the stability claim explicitly.
4. **Calibration drift bounded but not fixed.** Report the identified interval, multiplicative width and breakdown point. A point estimate alone is not the identified object.
5. **Calibration drift unbounded.** Do not make a directional channel claim. Measure the channel directly, calibrate the proxy in each regime or add a justified bound.
6. **Interval includes one.** The next observation should target calibration or the channel itself. Additional net-outcome samples reduce statistical uncertainty but not structural ambiguity.

These rules also clarify what a proxy-validation study must estimate. A high correlation between `X` and `F` within one regime is insufficient if the conversion changes across regimes. The relevant object is the ratio `q_1/q_0` on the comparison domain.

## 6. Scope and extensions

The model assumes positive multiplicative channels. Zeros require separate handling because ratios and division fail. Additional channels can be handled by measuring enough factors or bounding multiple calibration ratios, but the identified set becomes higher-dimensional. Nonmultiplicative interactions require a different structural map. Stochastic measurement error can be layered around the identified set, producing uncertainty about its endpoints, but it does not replace the calibration assumption.

The result is pointwise in `z`. Uniform conclusions over a trait domain require the interval to exclude one at every predeclared `z`, or require an explicitly defined aggregate estimand. Choosing the trait domain or calibration bound after inspecting outcomes would invalidate the intended sensitivity interpretation.

## 7. Discussion

The main lesson is that structural non-identifiability is not binary once scientifically defensible bounds are available. N3 and N4 are limiting cases. Between them lies a partial-identification region that can be reported and used for design.

The identified interval provides three pieces of information at once: the range of channel changes compatible with the data and calibration bound, the multiplicative uncertainty induced by that bound, and the point at which a directional conclusion fails. This changes the role of negative identification results. Rather than ending with `the channels may differ arbitrarily`, the analysis can state exactly which calibration departures matter.

The result is universal only over its stated problem class. It does not imply that every ecological outcome can be factorised as `W=FE`. The factorisation must be earned for a declared output and census interval. Once it is, however, N1–N4 and the bounded-drift interval do not depend on a particular taxon, island system or ecological mechanism.

A companion methodological programme can retain multiple admissible mechanisms and choose the next observation when the model family is too complex for a closed-form decomposition. That downstream problem is distinct. The present contribution is the boundary itself: what the available observation class can identify, what remains only partially identified, and what must be measured to cross the boundary.
