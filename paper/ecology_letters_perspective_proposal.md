# Ecology Letters Perspective proposal

Status: pre-submission proposal candidate. Current Ecology Letters guidance requires an unsolicited Perspective proposal to be no more than 300 words and to describe the nature and novelty of the work and its contribution to the discipline before a full manuscript is considered.

## Proposed title

**When ecological products hide mechanism: identification and measurement design for ecological chains**

## Proposal (271 words)

Ecologists routinely infer mechanism from composite quantities: visitation multiplied by per-visit effectiveness, seed number multiplied by post-dispersal quality, or endpoint responses linked through several unobserved stages. Yet a well-measured product can be structurally uninformative about how change is allocated among its components. We propose a Perspective that imports a simple identification lens into ecological measurement design and develops its consequences quantitatively.

For a positive `k`-stage product `W=prod_j F_j`, net-only observation leaves a `(k-1)`-dimensional product-preserving equivalence class in log space. Each independent direct channel anchor removes one dimension; `r` anchors leave `k-1-r` unresolved dimensions, and `k-1` anchors suffice for point identification. We then analyse a common proxy problem, `X_i=q_iF_i`. Bounding between-regime calibration transport by `1/Gamma <= q_1/q_0 <= Gamma` produces a sharp joint identified set, with stable calibration and unrestricted drift as limiting cases. A reference-invariant breakdown factor reports the minimum calibration distortion that overturns a directional conclusion, while the joint geometry shows why channel uncertainties generated by one calibration ratio cannot be reported as independent error bars.

Pollination provides an immediate ecological application: effective service depends on interaction quantity and direct effectiveness, so network degree, abundance or visitation alone cannot generally stand in for service. Longer change -> service -> dependency/assurance -> response chains expose the same missing-link problem.

The Perspective would synthesize these results into a general workflow linking observation-map choice, equivalence dimension, sensitivity analysis and the number/type of measurements required for identification. Accompanying open-source code and tested examples make the framework transferable across ecological systems. The author has developed the accompanying open-source identification and observation-design implementation and its reproducible test suite.
