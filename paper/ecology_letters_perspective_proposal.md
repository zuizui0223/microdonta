# Ecology Letters Perspective proposal

Status: pre-submission proposal candidate. Current Ecology Letters guidance requires an unsolicited Perspective proposal to be one paragraph, no more than 300 words, describing the nature and novelty of the work and its contribution to the discipline before a full manuscript is considered. The journal's editorial guidance also recommends stating the qualifications of the proposed author(s).

## Proposed title

**When ecological products hide mechanism: identification and measurement design for ecological chains**

## Proposal

Ecologists routinely infer mechanism from composite quantities: visitation multiplied by per-visit effectiveness, seed number multiplied by post-dispersal quality, or endpoint responses linked through several unobserved stages. Yet a well-measured product can be structurally uninformative about how change is allocated among its components. This Perspective develops an identification lens for ecological measurement design and its quantitative consequences. For a positive `k`-stage product `W=prod_j F_j`, net-only observation leaves a `(k-1)`-dimensional product-preserving equivalence class in log space; `r` independent direct channel anchors leave `k-1-r` unresolved dimensions, and `k-1` anchors suffice for point identification. For the common proxy problem `X_i=q_iF_i`, bounding between-regime calibration transport by `1/Gamma <= q_1/q_0 <= Gamma` gives a sharp joint identified set, with stable calibration and unrestricted drift as limiting cases. A reference-invariant breakdown factor reports the minimum calibration distortion that overturns a directional conclusion, while the joint geometry shows why uncertainties generated by one calibration ratio cannot be reported as independent error bars. Pollination provides an immediate application: effective service depends on interaction quantity and direct effectiveness, so network degree, abundance or visitation alone cannot generally stand in for service; longer change -> service -> dependency/assurance -> response chains expose the same missing-link problem. The Perspective synthesizes these results into a general workflow linking observation maps, equivalence dimension, sensitivity analysis and the measurements required for identification across ecological systems. The author has developed the accompanying open-source identification implementation and reproducible test suite that instantiate the proposed framework.
