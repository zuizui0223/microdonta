# Ecology Letters Perspective — editorial desk-rejection audit

Status: send-gate audit for the boundary-paper proposal.

## Editorial question 1 — Why is this a Perspective rather than a Method?

**Risk.** The proposal contains equations, code and measurement rules, so it can be read as a statistical/methods contribution.

**Required answer.** The paper changes how ecological evidence is classified before any particular estimator is chosen. Its central Perspective claim is that biological proximity to a process and identification among competing mechanisms are distinct properties. The product/proxy theorems quantify this distinction in recurring ecological observation maps. The code instantiates the perspective; it is not the primary contribution.

**Send criterion.** The proposal must explicitly distinguish mechanistic proximity from mechanistic identification and state that the claim applies before estimator choice.

## Editorial question 2 — What is genuinely new if the algebra is elementary?

**Risk.** `W=prod_j F_j` and proxy-calibration algebra are elementary and connect to established identifiability/partial-identification traditions.

**Required answer.** Novelty is the ecological evidentiary reclassification plus its closed quantitative consequences: product-preserving equivalence dimension (`k-1-r`), calibration-transport sensitivity and breakdown, and the resulting field-design/reporting rules. The claim is not that identifiability algebra is new, but that biological measurement level should not be used as a substitute for identification strength.

**Send criterion.** The proposal must state novelty positively without selling mathematical depth.

## Editorial question 3 — Is the scope broad enough for general ecology?

**Risk.** Pollination could make the paper look like a subfield critique of visitation/network proxies.

**Required answer.** Pollination is the lead concrete example; seed dispersal provides an independent quantity-by-quality architecture, genomic/molecular and field-experimental examples motivate the conceptual distinction, and the general evidentiary principle applies whenever explicit competing mechanisms map to observations. The formal `k-1-r` theorem is restricted to declared positive multiplicative chains.

**Send criterion.** Pollination must be an application, not the scope boundary, and the formal scope must not be overgeneralised beyond the stated observation maps.

## Editorial question 4 — Are we inventing a straw-man pattern-to-molecule hierarchy?

**Risk.** The strongest rhetoric can sound as if ecology has formally agreed that field data equal pattern and molecular data equal mechanism. The literature does not establish such a universal explicit hierarchy.

**Required answer.** The paper should describe two legitimate uses of *mechanistic* that can be conflated or left implicit: proximity to biological machinery and evidence that distinguishes process explanations. Ecological genomics explicitly pursues genetic mechanisms; Rudman et al. explicitly say genomic data can deepen mechanistic understanding while also noting that genomic data alone are not sufficient for the ecological questions considered. This is an ally to the distinction, not a target of criticism.

**Send criterion.** No sentence claims that ecology formally endorses a universal biological-depth hierarchy. Use “distinct” or “non-equivalent”, not a field-wide accusation.

## Editorial question 5 — Does this attack molecular or genomic ecology?

**Risk.** “Measurement level is not mechanism” could be misread as claiming that genomic or molecular data are non-mechanistic.

**Required answer.** Molecular and genomic measurements can be mechanistically proximal, experimentally manipulable and highly identifying. The paper makes only the conditional claim that proximity alone does not guarantee discrimination among the particular alternatives under study. Conversely, a field measurement can be strongly identifying when it directly anchors a missing channel or separates explicit alternatives. Smith et al. (2020) provides an independent example of field experiments testing mechanistic drivers under natural conditions.

**Send criterion.** The proposal/manuscript must contain both sides of the distinction: proximal measurements can be strongly informative, and field measurements can be strongly identifying. Never rank biological levels intrinsically.

## Editorial question 6 — Are the two axes really “orthogonal”?

**Risk.** The word *orthogonal* can imply statistical independence or zero empirical association between biological proximity and identification strength.

**Required answer.** That claim is unnecessary. The theory requires only that the two properties are non-equivalent and that no monotone relationship is assumed. They may be correlated in particular research programmes.

**Send criterion.** Primary text uses “distinct axes”, “non-equivalent properties” or “no monotone relation is assumed”. Do not make statistical-independence claims.

## Editorial question 7 — What changes for a field ecologist tomorrow?

**Risk.** Equivalence classes and evidentiary axes can sound abstract.

**Required answer.** Before collecting “deeper” data, investigators should declare competing mechanisms and ask which observation would distinguish them. For multiplicative chains, the framework says how many independent channel measurements are required (`k-1-r`); for transported proxies, it reports how much calibration failure overturns a directional conclusion; and it prohibits independently combinable channel error bars when one calibration ratio couples them.

**Send criterion.** At least one anchor-count consequence and one calibration/reporting consequence must appear in the proposal.

## Editorial question 8 — Is this just causal-inference relabelling?

**Risk.** Ecology now has active debates over causal effects and causal mechanisms, so the paper could look derivative of causal-inference terminology.

**Required answer.** The paper does not propose a causal-effect estimator and does not claim that identification of a latent decomposition is equivalent to all forms of causal identification. Its narrower object is the observation map: whether declared competing mechanisms or latent channel decompositions are observationally distinguishable under the measurements being used. Grace et al. (2025), Correia et al. (2025) and Siegel & Dee (2025) are positioned as adjacent design/evidence literatures, not as the same theorem.

**Send criterion.** Keep the target precise: mechanism discrimination / latent-channel identification conditional on a declared observation model.

## Editorial question 9 — Why now?

**Risk.** The problem is timeless, so the proposal may lack topical urgency.

**Required answer.** Contemporary ecology integrates field patterns with genomic, molecular, network and proxy measurements. That integration makes the distinction between *where* a measurement sits biologically and *what it identifies* especially consequential. Avoid unsupported trend counts; make the timeliness about cross-level evidence integration and changing-environment comparisons.

## Final send gate

The proposal is send-ready only if all are true:

- one paragraph;
- <=300 words;
- the two distinct axes are explicit: biological/mechanistic proximity and identification strength;
- no claim of statistical independence or a required monotone relationship between the axes;
- no claim that ecology formally endorses a universal field-to-molecule hierarchy;
- no intrinsic ranking of molecular/genomic versus field evidence;
- a proximal-but-non-identifying possibility and a field-but-identifying possibility are both acknowledged;
- nature, novelty, disciplinary contribution and author qualification explicit;
- Perspective-vs-Method distinction explicit;
- `k-1-r` anchor consequence present;
- `Gamma`/breakdown or equivalent calibration consequence present;
- pollination framed as an application, with an independent cross-domain architecture named;
- no claim of new identifiability algebra;
- no RACH/NOV/G2 claim;
- the source-by-source claims in `mechanistic_evidence_literature_audit.md` remain respected;
- email copy exactly matches the canonical proposal text;
- both current Ecology Letters Editorial Office recipients present.
